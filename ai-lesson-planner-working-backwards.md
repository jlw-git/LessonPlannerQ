# Working Backwards One-Pager: AI Lesson Planner

## Customer

The primary customer is a Buddhist educator teaching a small weekly class of 13-year-old students. The class has around four students, and each lesson is usually 90 minutes. The educator wants lessons that are meaningful, engaging, and relevant to students' daily lives.

The educator may be a volunteer or part-time teacher, not a trained curriculum designer. They care deeply about helping students apply Buddhist teachings, but they face limited preparation time, scattered source materials, and uncertainty about how to run play-based or self-directed learning well.

## Customer Problem

Every week, the educator has to create a new lesson plan from scratch. This is time-consuming because Buddhist teachings, especially within Chinese Mahayana folk Buddhism, are not organized into a single classroom-ready curriculum. The educator may need to consult sutras, stories, temple practices, festivals, commentaries, and oral traditions, then translate them into age-appropriate activities.

The educator also wants to move away from lecture-style teaching. They want students to explore, discuss, reflect, and apply teachings to real-life situations like friendship, anger, family expectations, school stress, compassion, gratitude, and social media behavior. However, self-directed learning feels intimidating because it requires careful scaffolding, clear prompts, resources, checkpoints, and reflection structures.

As a result, educators either spend too much time preparing, rely on repetitive lessons, or avoid richer teaching methods because they feel hard to execute.

## Customer Promise

The AI Lesson Planner helps Buddhist educators create a complete, ready-to-teach weekly lesson plan in minutes.

It turns scattered Buddhist source material into structured, age-appropriate lessons that combine Chinese Mahayana folk Buddhist teachings with play-based and scaffolded self-directed learning. The educator remains in control, but the product removes the blank-page burden.

The product also acts as a preparation coach. Educators can plan lessons by speaking naturally, rehearse difficult explanations before class, generate printable visual materials, and capture post-class reflections that improve the next lesson.

## Product Experience

The educator opens the AI Lesson Planner and enters a few details:

- lesson topic or value, such as compassion, karma, gratitude, mindfulness, filial piety, or repentance
- student age
- class size
- lesson duration, defaulting to 90 minutes
- preferred teaching style
- desired learning outcome
- source tradition, initially Chinese Mahayana folk Buddhism

The educator can also plan by voice. For example, they can say: "Next week I want to teach compassion, but my students are restless and I want an activity." The assistant asks follow-up questions, clarifies constraints, and then generates the lesson plan. This matters because many educators think through lessons aloud more naturally than they complete a form.

The product generates a complete weekly lesson package, including:

- lesson title
- Buddhist teaching or story
- learning objectives
- opening activity
- main activity
- play-based exercise
- self-directed learning task
- discussion prompts
- reflection questions
- real-life application exercise
- educator facilitation notes
- materials needed
- optional take-home activity

For self-directed learning, the product uses an established scaffold such as gradual release of responsibility: "I do, we do, you do." This lets the educator introduce the teaching, guide students through shared exploration, then allow students to apply the idea independently or in pairs.

The product can also generate classroom-ready visual materials, including:

- story cards
- scene illustrations
- reflection prompts
- classroom posters
- activity sheets
- scenario cards
- value cards
- role-play character cards
- board-game tiles
- matching cards for concepts and actions

For example, the educator can ask the product to create four comic-style panels showing a student choosing compassion during a school conflict. The product can also turn a Buddhist story into a visual sequence students can arrange, discuss, or reinterpret. This fits a class of four students especially well because each student can take one panel, role, or scene.

Before class, the educator can use the rehearsal coach to practice. For example, they can say: "Pretend you are a skeptical 13-year-old. Ask me hard questions about karma." The assistant simulates student responses, helps the educator practice explanations, and suggests simpler language.

During class, the product can act as an educator-facing live facilitation copilot. If students finish early, the educator can quietly ask: "Give me a 5-minute backup activity because the students finished early." The product should remain educator-facing at first, rather than allowing unsupervised student-agent interaction in the MVP.

After class, the educator can record a short reflection circle debrief. For example: "Students understood compassion but struggled with applying it at school." The product converts this into planning context, class memory, and suggested follow-up for the next lesson.

## Why Now

Educators increasingly want students to be active participants, not passive listeners. At the same time, volunteer and community-based educators often do not have the time or training to design high-quality lessons every week.

AI can now help convert scattered knowledge into structured teaching materials, but religious and values-based education needs more than generic content generation. It needs sensitivity to tradition, age-appropriate framing, and practical classroom design.

Realtime voice and image generation make the product more useful at the moments where educators feel the most friction: thinking through a lesson, preparing materials, responding live in class, and remembering what happened afterward.

## Why This Product Wins

Generic AI lesson planners can create activities quickly, but they usually miss the educator's deeper problem: Buddhist education is source-fragmented, tradition-sensitive, values-based, and often taught by volunteers who need confidence as much as content.

This product is better for this educator because it is purpose-built around the actual teaching context:

- It defaults to Chinese Mahayana folk Buddhism instead of treating Buddhism as one uniform subject.
- It designs for 90-minute small-group lessons rather than generic school-period plans.
- It emphasizes real-life application, not just content coverage.
- It scaffolds self-directed learning with "I do, We do, You do" instead of simply telling students to explore independently.
- It includes educator rehearsal, so the teacher can practice answering skeptical or emotionally sensitive questions before class.
- It generates visual and play-based materials for a class of four, reducing the educator's design and prep burden.
- It keeps the educator in control with review notes, tradition labels, and culturally sensitive image guidance.

## Customer Benefit

For educators, the product saves preparation time, reduces stress, and increases confidence in using play-based and self-directed learning.

For students, lessons become more engaging and personally relevant. Instead of only hearing about Buddhist values, students discuss and practice how those values apply to everyday life.

For Buddhist education groups, the product improves consistency of lesson quality while still allowing each educator to adapt lessons to their own temple, class, and teaching style.

The product also helps educators create richer classroom experiences without becoming designers or curriculum specialists. Illustrated worksheets can make reflection feel guided rather than exam-like, using prompts such as: "When I felt angry, what did I notice? What choice did I make?"

## MVP Scope

The first version focuses only on Chinese Mahayana folk Buddhism for 13-year-old students in small classes.

The MVP should support:

- weekly 90-minute lesson plan generation
- topic-based lesson creation
- voice-based lesson planning interview
- age-appropriate explanations
- play-based activities
- scaffolded self-directed learning activities
- educator rehearsal coach for practicing student questions and explanations
- educator notes
- real-life application prompts
- printable lesson visual packs and play-based learning materials
- illustrated student reflection worksheets
- editable lesson outputs

The MVP should not try to cover all Buddhist traditions at launch. It should avoid presenting Buddhism as a single uniform tradition.

The live facilitation copilot and reflection circle assistant can follow after the core preparation workflow is working reliably. If included early, they should remain educator-facing only.

## Success Metrics

- educators can generate a usable lesson plan in under 10 minutes
- weekly lesson preparation time is reduced by at least 50%
- educators rate generated lessons as useful or very useful
- educators reuse or adapt generated lessons across multiple weeks
- educators report that generated 90-minute plans are easier to facilitate than generic AI-generated lesson plans
- educators use voice planning or rehearsal before class
- educators generate at least one printable or visual material for a lesson
- students can explain how a Buddhist teaching applies to daily life
- educators report increased confidence using self-directed learning

## Risks And Open Questions

The product must handle Buddhist teachings respectfully and avoid oversimplifying doctrine. It must distinguish Chinese Mahayana folk Buddhist framing from other Buddhist traditions.

Source quality matters. Over time, the product should move toward a curated content library rather than relying only on open-ended generation.

Self-directed learning may still feel unfamiliar to educators. The product should therefore include facilitation guidance, not just student activities.

Realtime features should be introduced carefully. The first version should use voice to support the educator's planning and rehearsal, not to replace adult facilitation or create unsupervised student conversations.

Image generation must be culturally sensitive. Visuals involving Buddhas, bodhisattvas, temples, rituals, or offerings should use respectful style presets and require educator review before classroom use.

## Future Roadmap

After proving the weekly Buddhist lesson planner, the product can expand into a broader curriculum assistant.

Next phases could include a curated Buddhist content library, student worksheets, reflection journals, festival-based lesson packs, multi-week curriculum planning, realtime live facilitation support, post-class voice debriefs, and eventually support for other subjects such as values education, culture, history, language learning, and character education.
